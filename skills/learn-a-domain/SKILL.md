# Learn a domain

People learn a field by doing small real things in it, guessing what will happen, and getting quick, honest feedback, not by collecting summaries. This skill builds that loop for any subject, keeps every factual claim tied to a source the learner can check, and knows where it must stop and send them to a professional.

This is a guidance-only skill: no scripts, by design. `<skill>/templates/` has the plan and log formats; `<skill>/examples/` has a worked plan.

## 1. Classify the domain (say it out loud)

| Kind | Examples | Shape of the loop |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative practice** | drawing, music, writing, cooking, photography, typography | Short exercises, make something every session, compare with references, critique, repeat. Taste is the learner's; offer contrasting examples, never one "correct" style. |
| **Factual study** | history, biology, economics, a language's grammar, a standard | Sources first, predict-then-check questions, explain back, spaced recall. Every factual claim cites a source. |
| **Professional advice needed** | diagnosis or treatment, legal action, investing or tax decisions, electrical or structural work, anything with safety risk | Teach concepts and vocabulary only, from reputable sources, so the learner can talk to a professional. Say plainly what needs one, and do not give individual advice. |

Many subjects mix kinds (learning about nutrition is factual; changing a medical diet needs a professional). Mark each part.

## 2. Pin down the goal

Ask one question at a time: what they want to be able to *do* (not "know"), by when, how much time per week, what they already know, and what materials or tools they have. Write a one-line goal: "Sight-read simple 4/4 rhythms on a hand drum in 4 weeks, 20 minutes a day."

## 3. Find sources, then plan

Pick 2 to 4 sources the learner can open (a standard textbook, an official body, a respected practitioner's material). Prefer primary or authoritative ones and say why each was chosen. If you could not verify a source, say so. Then write `learning-plan.md` from `templates/learning-plan.md`: 4 to 8 sessions, each with one idea, one worked example, one exercise with a visible result, and one prediction to make before checking.

## 4. Each session

1. **Idea** in plain words, with the source.
2. **Worked example**, start to finish.
3. **Predict**: the learner guesses the outcome of the exercise first. Wrong guesses are the useful part; log them in `prediction-log.md`.
4. **Do** the exercise and look at the real result (a recording, a drawing, an answer checked against the source).
5. **Feedback**: what matched, what did not, one concrete thing to change next time.
6. **Explain back** in two or three sentences; tidy their words rather than replacing them.

## 5. Close the loop

After the plan, write a one-page summary in their words, the predictions they got wrong and what they learned from them, and 5 review questions for spaced recall (tomorrow, 3 days, a week). Hand off to teach-back if they want a deeper check.

## Rules

- No fact without a source the learner can open. If unsure, say so.
- Practice over reading: every session ends with something made or answered.
- Never mine the user's private files or conversations to guess their learning style; use only what they tell or give you.
- In professional-advice areas, teach the map, not the decision.
