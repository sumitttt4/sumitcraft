# Learning plan: reading rhythm on a hand drum (worked example)

A neutral example to show the format. The sources named are kinds of source to look for, not endorsements; check the
current edition and author before use.

- **Goal:** sight-read and play simple 4/4 rhythms (whole to eighth notes, quarter rests) at 80 bpm without stopping.
- **By / time:** 4 weeks, 20 minutes a day.
- **Domain kind:** creative practice (playing) with a factual core (notation). Nothing needs a professional, except
  pain in the wrists or hands, which is a reason to stop and ask a teacher or clinician.
- **Starting point:** can keep a steady beat clapping; cannot read notation.
- **Sources:** a beginner rhythm-reading method book (graded exercises), a metronome app, a music-theory reference for
  note values (to check every definition).

| # | Idea | Worked example | Exercise (visible result) | Prediction first | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A bar of 4/4 holds four quarter-note beats | Count "1 2 3 4" over four quarter notes | Record 8 bars of quarters at 60 bpm | "Will I drift ahead of the click?" | theory reference: time signatures |
| 2 | Half and whole notes last 2 and 4 beats | Count and play half-half, then whole | Record a 4-bar mixed line | "Which bar will I rush?" | method book, unit 1 |
| 3 | Rests are counted silence | Quarter rest on beat 2 | Play a line with rests on 2 and 4 | "Will I come in early after the rest?" | method book, unit 2 |
| 4 | Eighths split a beat: "1 and 2 and" | Count aloud while tapping | Record 4 bars of eighths at 70 bpm | "Where will the evenness break?" | method book, unit 3 |
| 5 | Sight-reading: read ahead one bar | Mark the hardest bar first | Play an unseen 8-bar line once, no stopping | "How many mistakes?" (write a number) | method book, sight-reading pages |

Feedback each session comes from listening to the recording against the click, not from feeling.
